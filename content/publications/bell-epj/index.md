### Supplementary Notes & Key Findings

* [cite_start]**Educational Purpose**: The document details an accessible, hands-on experimental setup designed to help undergraduate and master's students understand non-intuitive quantum concepts like superposition and entanglement[cite: 2120, 2122, 2882].
* [cite_start]**Photon Production**: The setup generates entangled photon pairs via spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) utilizing two type I BBO crystals illuminated by a 405 nm laser[cite: 2325, 2403, 2418]. 
* **Detection Configurations**: The authors provide two variations of the setup:
    * *Setup 1* uses two input channels of a 4-channel detector, employing a quarter-wave plate and linear polarizer. [cite_start]It is optically simpler but slower for taking measurements[cite: 2386, 2387].
    * [cite_start]*Setup 2* utilizes all four channels along with polarizing beam splitters (PBS), which requires more optical elements but significantly speeds up the measurement process[cite: 2390, 2391].
* [cite_start]**Setup Capabilities**: A defining feature of these proposed setups is their ability to precisely control the relative phase, allowing for the deliberate generation of all four maximally entangled Bell states[cite: 2320, 2321].
* **Experimental Results**:
    * [cite_start]**Tomography**: Full quantum state tomography was successfully performed on the produced states, consistently achieving a fidelity higher than 88% compared to the theoretically expected states[cite: 2844, 2871].
    * [cite_start]**Bell Test**: The experiments successfully yielded violations of the CHSH inequalities by at least 40 standard deviations, clearly demonstrating the non-local behavior of the entangled photons[cite: 2874, 2893].
