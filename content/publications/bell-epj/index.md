abstract: "The growth of quantum technologies is attracting the interest of many students eager to learn concepts such as quantum entanglement or quantum superposition. However, the non-intuitive nature of these concepts poses a challenge to understanding them. Here, we present an entangled photon system which can perform a Bell test, i.e. the CHSH inequality, and can obtain the complete tomography of the two-photon state. The proposed setup is versatile, cost-effective and allows for multiple classroom operating modes. We present two variants, both facilitating the measurement of Bell inequalities and quantum state tomography. Experimental results showcase successful manipulation of the quantum state of the photons, achieving high-fidelity entangled states and significant violations of Bell's inequalities. Our setup's simplicity and affordability enhances accessibility for less specialized laboratories, allowing students to familiarize themselves with quantum physics concepts."

### Supplementary Notes & Key Findings

* **Educational Purpose**: The document details an accessible, hands-on experimental setup designed to help undergraduate and master's students understand non-intuitive quantum concepts like superposition and entanglement.
* **Photon Production**: The setup generates entangled photon pairs via spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) utilizing two type I BBO crystals illuminated by a 405 nm laser. 
* **Detection Configurations**: The authors provide two variations of the setup:
    * *Setup 1* uses two input channels of a 4-channel detector, employing a quarter-wave plate and linear polarizer. It is optically simpler but slower for taking measurements.
    * *Setup 2* utilizes all four channels along with polarizing beam splitters (PBS), which requires more optical elements but significantly speeds up the measurement process.
* **Setup Capabilities**: A defining feature of these proposed setups is their ability to precisely control the relative phase, allowing for the deliberate generation of all four maximally entangled Bell states.
* **Experimental Results**:
    * **Tomography**: Full quantum state tomography was successfully performed on the produced states, consistently achieving a fidelity higher than 88% compared to the theoretically expected states.
    * **Bell Test**: The experiments successfully yielded violations of the CHSH inequalities by at least 40 standard deviations, clearly demonstrating the non-local behavior of the entangled photons.
